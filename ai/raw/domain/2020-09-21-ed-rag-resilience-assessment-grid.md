## Notes

- The study redesigns Hollnagel's resilience assessment grid (RAG) into an emergency-department version, the ED-RAG.
- Step 1 of the method: translation of Hollnagel's RAG into a Chinese version, then a tailored question set for emergency departments.
- The field test took place in a private regional hospital.
- The resilience definition that the article uses requires "the potentials to respond, monitor, anticipate, and learn".
- The article describes each potential:
  - respond: what an organization needs to respond timely and effectively to what happens.
  - monitor: how well the organization detects changes to work conditions, and the indicators it uses to keep track of what happens.
  - learn: the learning bases and targets, when learning takes place, and how the effects of learning are verified.
  - anticipate: the ability to look ahead and to consider future events, conditions, threats and opportunities.
- Hollnagel developed the RAG as four sets of generic questions, one set per potential. The answers build a resilience profile for each of the four potentials. The RAG must be adjusted to the organization that is studied.
- The original RAG rates on five classes: excellent (5), satisfactory (4), acceptable (3), unacceptable (2), and deficient (1).
- The research team could not agree on definitions of the five classes. Insufficient evidence at the initial stage could hardly be classified into five levels. So the ED-RAG uses a four-point (1 to 4) scale with no pre-defined names for the levels. Level 4 is the relatively best performance among the answers. It "does not mean excellent".
- The ED-RAG version 5 has 38 questions corresponding to 32 foci. An interview takes around 2 hours.
- Scores are posted on a radar chart per potential. The area of the polygon is the standardized score of the potential. The order of the axes changes the area, so the study fixed the order to ensure reproducibility.
- Caution in the discussion: the intention of the RAG "was never to use it as a rating tool", neither for one unit nor to compare units. A single score does not show where the problems are or what to do about them. The RAG gives a relative profile to compare over time, not an absolute rating.
